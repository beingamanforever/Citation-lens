Citation Lens

Literature research for Codex and Claude Code. Find the defining papers, walk their citation graph in both directions, surface the newest work, and cite only links you can check.

5 MCP toolsSemantic Scholar + OpenAlex + arXivCards, not documentsAgent stays in charge

Web search finds pages. Research needs a graph.

Ask an agent for the literature on a topic and it searches the web, opens a few pages and lists what it saw. Citation Lens gives it what a researcher uses instead: a citation graph. Start from a few papers you trust, and see what they build on, what builds on them, and what appeared last month.

1 · SEARCH

Search wide, once

2–4 query variants on three scholarly indexes at the same time, merged and ranked together. Every third card is recent work.

2 · CHOOSE

Pick the seeds

The agent chooses 3–6 papers that define the topic. That judgment stays with the agent; Lens only does the gathering.

3 · WALK

Walk the citation graph

References, citing papers and similar papers of every seed, ranked by shared references and co-citation into three lanes.

4 · READ

Read only what matters

Compact cards with a quotable abstract sentence first. Full abstracts, text and figures on demand, never all at once.

Foundation. Older papers the seeds cite, ranked by how many on-topic papers in the graph also cite them.

Follow-up. Papers that cite or resemble the seeds, ranked by shared references (bibliographic coupling).

Recent. Work from the last two years, including preprints too new to have citation counts.

Every paper each answer listed

A citation link counts as verified only when the cited paper appears in the citing paper's own reference list (arXiv bibliography or a scholarly index). Agents often claim far more links than they can support, so the graphs draw verified links only. Counts come from the independent grader in evals/grade.py.
